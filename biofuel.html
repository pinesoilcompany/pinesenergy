What We Do

Biodiesel for the Working Farm

Pines BioFuel is the production and distribution branch of Pines Energy Group LLC. It converts used cooking oil collected through the company's Pines Oil Recycling operation into finished biodiesel fuel, then delivers that fuel directly to agricultural customers throughout East Texas.

Farms and ranches represent the primary customer base. These operations run diesel-powered equipment daily. Tractors, generators, irrigation pumps, and transport vehicles all consume fuel at rates that make price volatility a real operational risk. Pines BioFuel addresses that risk by offering pricing tied to regional feedstock costs rather than crude oil futures.

The result is a fuel supply relationship that is more predictable, more local, and measurably cleaner than conventional petroleum diesel when blended and used correctly.

Equipment Compatibility

Low and mid-level biodiesel blends are compatible with most standard diesel engines, while higher blends may require fuel system, storage, seal, warranty, and cold-flow review. We advise customers on blend selection based on equipment inventory and operating conditions.

Why Biodiesel

Advantages Over Petroleum Diesel

Biodiesel is not an experimental fuel. It has been used in agricultural and commercial fleet operations for decades. The practical advantages for farm and ranch operations are well documented.

01

Price Stability

Biodiesel pricing at Pines BioFuel is based on regional feedstock costs and production overhead, not crude oil futures traded on global commodity markets. When crude prices spike, farm fuel budgets absorb the impact. Biodiesel supply from a regional producer decouples that risk.

02

Cleaner Combustion

Biodiesel burns more completely than petroleum diesel. Particulate matter, carbon monoxide, and unburned hydrocarbon emissions are measurably reduced. Equipment operating on biodiesel blends typically shows cleaner exhaust and reduced carbon buildup in fuel systems over time.

03

Lubricity

Biodiesel has strong lubricating properties compared to ultra-low sulfur diesel. Even low blend ratios such as B2 or B5 can improve lubricity, while B10 and B20 provide practical higher-biodiesel options for many operations.

04

No Engine Modification

Common blends such as B5, B10, and B20 generally require no engine modifications in current and recent diesel-powered farm equipment. Blend selection should still consider manufacturer guidance, storage practices, and seasonal temperatures.

05

Regional Sourcing

The feedstock for Pines BioFuel comes from used cooking oil collected within the same regional territory as the customer base. Money spent on fuel stays within the regional economy rather than leaving to distant commodity suppliers or national fuel distributors.

06

Reduced Carbon Output

Biodiesel derived from waste cooking oil carries a significantly lower lifecycle carbon footprint than petroleum diesel. The feedstock would otherwise be disposed of as waste. Diverting it to fuel production avoids both the disposal burden and the emissions associated with producing an equivalent volume of fossil fuel.

How It's Made

From Collected Oil to Finished Fuel

Biodiesel production follows an established chemical process that converts triglycerides in vegetable and cooking oils into fatty acid methyl esters (FAME) suitable for use in diesel engines.

1

Collection

Used cooking oil is picked up from restaurant accounts throughout the East Texas service region using company-owned vehicles.

2

Filtration

Raw oil is filtered to remove food solids and water content before entering the processing stage. Feedstock quality affects final fuel quality.

3

Transesterification

Filtered oil reacts with methanol in the presence of a catalyst. This process separates glycerol from the oil, producing raw biodiesel and glycerol as a byproduct.

4

Quality and Delivery

Finished biodiesel is tested, blended to specification, and delivered directly to farm and ranch customers across the service territory.

Fuel Supply Program

Becoming a Fuel Customer

Pines BioFuel works directly with farm, ranch, and fleet operations to establish scheduled delivery arrangements. The process begins with an assessment of the customer's equipment inventory, monthly fuel consumption, storage setup, and preferred blend range.

  • 01
    Initial Consultation We review your equipment type, diesel consumption volume, and delivery access to determine the appropriate blend and delivery schedule.
  • 02
    Blend Selection Most customers begin with common blends such as B5, B10, or B20. Higher blends are reviewed for compatible operations. Blend guidance is provided at no cost.
  • 03
    Supply Agreement Long-term supply contracts are available, providing price predictability for both parties. Month-to-month arrangements are also supported depending on volume.
  • 04
    Scheduled Delivery Fuel is delivered on a scheduled basis directly to your property. No transload, no third-party driver, no brokered delivery.
